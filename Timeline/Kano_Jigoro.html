	timeline
	title Timeline of Kanō Jigorō
	Section Foundations of Judo
	December 10, 1860: Born in Mikage, Hyōgo, Japan (now part of Kobe). His father, Kanō Jirōsaku, was a sake brewer and a lay Confucian scholar.
	1871: Moves to Tokyo with his father after his mother’s death.
	1874: Attends various preparatory schools, excelling in Western studies, English, and Chinese classics.
	1877: Enrolls at Tokyo Imperial University; studies political science and philosophy.
	: Began his martial arts journey with Tenjin Shin'yō-ryū jujutsu under Fukuda Hachinosuke.
	1880:  After Fukuda's death, he studied under Iso Masatomo
	1881: Graduated from Tokyo Imperial University (now University of Tokyo)
	: Begins training in Kito-ryū jujutsu under Tsunetoshi Iikubo.
	Section Early years of judo
	May 1882: Founded Kōdōkan (講道館), meaning "Institute for the Study of the Way". A 12-tatami room at the Eishōji Temple in Tokyo.
	1883: Promoted to Menkyo (license) in Kito-ryū; awards the first judo black belts (Shodan) to Shiro Saigo and Tsunejirō Tomita.
	1884: Establishes Kōdōkan Judo Rank System (first martial art to use dan/kyū grades).
	1886: The Tokyo Police jujutsu tournament is held. Kodokan won 13 of the 15 matches, with the remaining two ending in draws. Kanō hand-picked team members: Shiro Saigo, Tsunejirō Tomita, Yamashita Yoshiaki and Yokoyama Sakujiro
	: This victory led to judo being adopted by the Tokyo Metropolitan Police as the official martial art for training, marking a major milestone in its national acceptance.
	1887–1888: Kōdōkan expands rapidly; judoka like Shiro Saigo gain fame.
	1889: Traveled to Europe to promote judo and observe Western education systems.
	Section Judo as education
	1891: Appointed professor at the Gakushuin (Peers’ School).
	1893: Becomes principal of Tokyo Higher Normal School
	1895: Kōdōkan becomes the official headquarters of judo, codifying techniques and ranks.
	1898–1901: Director of Primary Education at the Ministry of Education
	1899: Starts introducing judo as part of Japan’s national school curriculum
	May 1906: Sanpo Toku joins the Kodokan and begins training directly under Kanō Jigorō.
	Section Internation expansion of Judo
	1909: Appointed first Asian member of the International Olympic Committee (IOC).
	1910: Establishes the Japan Amateur Sports Association to promote organized sports.
	1911: Forms the Japan Amateur Athletic Association, uniting dojos under Kōdōkan governance.
	1912: Japan participates in the Stockholm Olympics, influenced by Kanō’s IOC efforts.
	: Kanō forced to expel Sanpo Toku from the Kōdōkan due to a fight with 15 sailors of the Brazilian Navy
	1917: Toku was pardoned by Kano and reinstated as a Kodokan teacher
	1921: Begins touring Europe and North America, promoting judo and Japanese culture.
	1922: Establishes the Kōdōkan Cultural Society to promote the deeper philosophical goals of judo.
	1926: Publishes lectures and essays on Seiryoku Zen’yō and Jita Kyōei.
	1933: Helps found the International Judo Federation (IJF).
	Section Final years
	1934: Attends IOC meetings and promotes Tokyo as a potential Olympic host city.
	1935: Receives the Order of the Sacred Treasure, Gold and Silver Star, from the Japanese government.
	1936: Judo begins to be practiced worldwide, notably in Europe, Brazil, and the U.S.
	May 4, 1938: Dies of pneumonia aboard the Hikawa Maru while returning from an IOC meeting in Cairo.
	Section Legacy
	Posthumous: Grand Cordon of the Order of the Rising Sun
: Recognized as the father of judo and a pioneer of modern physical education in Japan.
	1964: Judo becomes an Olympic sport at the Tokyo Summer Olympics, fulfilling Kanō’s dream.
