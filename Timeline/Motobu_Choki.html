	title Timeline of Motobu Chōki 

	Section Okinawa - Early years
	April 5, 1870: Born in Akahira Village, Shuri, Ryūkyū Kingdom (present-day Okinawa, Japan), to a branch of the Ryukyuan royal family. He was the third son of Lord Motobu Chōshin so initially not given the same education in martial arts as his older brothers.
	Around 1880: Observed and imitated his older brother Motobu Chōyū, a skilled martial artist, though he was not formally allowed to train with him.
	: Began self-training by striking makiwara and lifting stones to build strength.
	1882: At the age of 12, secretly he begins his karate training under Ankō Itosu, Sōkon Matsumura, and Kōsaku Matsumora
	1889: By the age of 19, he continues his training under Kōsaku Matsumora, along with his older brother Chōyū and friend Kentsū Yabu
	: Practiced Naihanchi (Tekki) kata extensively, believing it held the key to real combat.
	Early 1900s: Became well-known throughout Okinawa for his practical karate techniques and exceptional kumite(shiai) skills, earning the nickname "Motobu no Saru" (Motobu the Monkey) for his agility
	: Likely worked in law enforcement or security roles, where his skills were put to practical use.

	Section First visit to mainland Japan
	1908: Traveled to Osaka, Japan, seeking better opportunities.
	1920s: Kori Hisataka became one of his Japanese students, during the time he was actively teaching in Osaka.
	1921: At the age of 51, he moves to mainland Japan to promote karate
	1922: Defeats a Western boxer in Kyoto, which significantly boosts his reputation in Japan
	: Soon after, misleading articles depicted Gichin Funakoshi (rather than Motobu) as the karate expert responsible for the victory.
	1923: Establishment of his first dojo in Osaka
	: Taught mostly Japanese students, including Tatetsu Kinjō and Sannosuke Ueshima.
	1925: Public criticism of Gichin Funakoshi, claiming it was ineffective for real fighting.
	: Funakoshi claimed Motobu was crude due to his limited education and strong Okinawan dialect.
	1926: Publishes his first book, "Okinawa Kenpō Karate-jutsu Kumite-hen,"(Kumite Edition of Okinawa Kenpō Karate Art) focusing on kumite techniques
	1927: Moves to Tokyo, establishes the Daidōkan dojo, and becomes the first Shihan of the karate club at Tokyo University
	January 7, 1927: He demonstrated wood-board breaking and kumite in front of Kanō Jigorō, who was visiting Okinawa. He was introduced as "Okinawa's Number One" in kumite and was greatly admired by Kanō.
	1930s: Teaches notable students including Yasuhiro Konishi, Hironori Ōtsuka, and boxer "Piston" Horiguchi
	1932: Publishes his second book, "Watashi no Karate-jutsu," (My karate art) which includes his philosophy and training methods (including how to apply Naihanchi-based movements in combat).

	Section Temporary return to Okinawa
	Fall 1936: Struggled to gain widespread recognition compared to Funakoshi’s structured Shotokan karate,  he temporarily closes his Daidōkan dojo in Tokyo and he moved back to Okinawa, where he continued teaching privately.
	October 25 1936: Participates in the famous meeting of Okinawan karate masters in Naha, which discusses the future of karate
	November 1936: Attended a roundtable discussion organized by young Okinawan karate practitioners. He was invited as the most practical karate master.
	Section Return to mainland Japan
	1937: Returns to Osaka and begins teaching karate to his son, Motobu Chōsei, occasionally traveled to Daidōkan dojo in Tokyo to teach karate
	Fall 1941: Closes his Tokyo dojo due to deteriorating health and returns to Osaka

	Section Final return to Okinawa
	June 1942: Briefly teaches karate at Tottori Agriculture High School (now Tottori University Faculty of Agriculture)
	April 15, 1944: Passes away in Shuri, Okinawa, at the age of 74.
