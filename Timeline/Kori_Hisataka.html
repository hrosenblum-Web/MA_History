timeline
    title Kōri Hisataka (1907–1988) — Life and Karate Legacy

    April 22, 1907 : Born in Shuri, Okinawa
         : Exposed to traditional Okinawan martial culture

    1920s : Begins formal karate training
          : Becomes a direct student of Chōtoku Kyan
          : Studies Shuri-te / Shōrin-ryū principles

    Late 1920s–1930s : Further budō exposure
                     : Influenced by kendō and jūdō
                     : Develops interest in structured, safe training methods

    Pre-1945 : Moves to mainland Japan
             : Teaches karate prior to and during WWII
             : Adapts Okinawan karate to Japanese budō environment

    Post-1945 : Resumes teaching after WWII
              : Focuses on rebuilding karate as a budō discipline
              : Begins formal systematization of his teachings

    Late 1940s–1950s : Founds Shōrinji-ryū Kenkōkan Karatedō
                      : Emphasizes health (kenkō), realism, and longevity
                      : Preserves Kyan-ha kata and principles

    1950s–1960s : Develops bōgu kumite
                 : Adapts kendō armor for karate sparring
                 : Enables full-speed, full-power training with reduced injury

    1960s–1970s : Kenkōkan spreads within Japan
                 : Style gains recognition for realism and safety
                 : Influences later full-contact and jissen karate approaches

    1980s : Oversees succession planning
          : Son Masayuki (Masayoshi) Hisataka assumes increasing leadership
          : Kenkōkan expands internationally

    August 14, 1988 : Death of Kōri Hisataka (Tokyo, Japan)
         : Legacy continues through Kenkōkan organizations worldwide
