	title Timeline of Masahiko Kimura
	Section Early years
	Sept. 10, 1917: Born in Kumamoto, on Kyushu Island, Japan.
	1926: Started training in Judo, under Kinpei Gima, a local judo instructor and early student of Jigoro Kano, and Miyake Tsunejiro.
	1930: Awarded Shodan
	: Shows early signs of extraordinary dedication and strength.
	1931: Promoted to Yondan after defeating six 3rd and 4th dan opponents
	1932-1934: Attends Kumamoto Technical High School.
	: Engages in a fierce, high-volume training regimen.
	: Is known to perform 1,000 push-ups daily.
	: Reportedly throws his practice partners hundreds of times per session.
	1935: Awarded Godan (Youngest at that time) after defeating eight consecutive opponents at the Kodokan.
	: Suffered four rare losses in the same year, including defeats by Kenshiro Abe and Hideo Yamamoto
	: Considered quitting judo but resumed training with renewed intensity, practicing osoto gari against a tree and sparring daily.
	1936: Moves to Tokyo and enters Takushoku University.
	: trained under Tatsukuma Ushijima (Known for his strict training. e.g. forced to sleep in a barn as punishment.)
	Section Competitive career
	1937-1939: Won the All-Japan Championship three years in a row (first person to do so), which earned him the right to retire the championship flag
	1940s: Trained in karate under Gichin Funakoshi (Shotokan) and later So-Nei Chu (Goju-Ryu). Became an assistant instructor alongside Masutatsu Oyama and Gogen Yamaguchi
	: Won the Ten-Ran Shiai tournament
	1941: Graduated from Takushoku University and became an assistant in the Martial Arts department
	1941–1945: World War II disrupts many sporting events.
	: Kimura remains active in judo and training.
	: Reportedly never loses a match during this period.
	1943: Joined the Japanese Army, where he taught judo
	1947: Won the West Japan Judo Championship
	: Promoted to 7th dan, though his rank was later frozen due to disputes with the Kodokan
	1949: Wins All-Japan Championship again
	1950: Began participating in professional judo and wrestling, due to economic needs (motivated by his wife's illness and the need for expensive tuberculosis medication) and dwindling judo sponsorships, sometimes facing opponents from other martial arts.
	: Participated in a professional judo tour in Hawaii, where he also accepted challenge matches and performed demonstrations
	1951: Travels to Brazil with judoka/pro wrestler Yukio Kato and Kiyojiro Matsumoto to participate in several judo exhibitions and pro wrestling matches.
	October 23, 1951: Kimura faced Hélio Gracie (BJJ) in a highly publicized no-holds-barred challenge match in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil.
	: Kimura defeated Gracie using a gyaku ude-garami (reverse arm lock). Gracie refused to tap, and his arm was broken before his corner (Carlos Gracie) throws in the towel. BJJ still refer to this technique as the "Kimura".
	1952-1964: Joins the Japanese professional wrestling scene.
	: Travels to the United States and Hawaii, competing in pro wrestling circuits.
	: Had bouts with “Judo” Gene LeBell and Lou Thesz
	1954: Wrestles against Hārū Kōji in a match that blends real grappling and pro wrestling.
	: Faces Rikidōzan (former sumo and top Japanese wrestler) in a controversial match. Allegedly Rikidōzan double-crossed Kimura during a supposed scripted match. Rikidōzan attacks full-force; Kimura suffers injuries.
	: Despite commercial success, Kimura becomes ostracized by the Kodokan, which frowns upon participation in professional wrestling.
	Section Later in life
	1955-1958: Retired from judo competition and began extensive international tours, teaching and wrestling in Mexico, France, London, Spain, and Latin America, further spreading his influence
	1960-1980: Returned to Japan, taught judo at Tokushoku University, and mentored several world-class judoka
	: Despite his victories, Kimura faced challenges in his later life, including being marginalized by the Kodokan due to his involvement in professional wrestling.
	: Continues to coach and write about judo.
	: Conducts seminars and exhibitions internationally.
	: Coaches students in Japan and abroad, emphasizing traditional judo values and conditioning.
	1968: Published Judo Kyoshitsu (Judo Classroom) — instructional book on judo techniques
	1977: Published Judo:Research into Techniques Useful in Actual Combat — explores practical judo applications
	: Published Judo für Anfänger und Kämpfer (Judo for beginners and fighters)
	1991: Published an autobiography titled "My Judo" (Japanese title: "Waga Judo"), which gives insight into his philosophy and training.
	1992: Diagnosed with lung cancer. Keeps training and teaching as long as possible despite illness.
	April 18, 1993: Passed away after battling lung cancer.
	Section Legacy
	2014: Published NEWAZA:Revealing the Secrets of Kosen Judo Grappling Techniques
	2016: Published Everything You Need to Know About Judo Ukemi — a guide to breakfall techniques
	2018: Published Judo Technique — a technical manual — focused on ground grappling techniques
	Section Famous students
	Close Disciples: Katsuhiko Kashiwazaki - Trained directly with Kimura; absorbed his newaza style
	: Satoru Sayama (Tiger Mask) - Mentored by Kimura in realistic grappling
	: Yoshinori Nishi - Assisted Kimura in pro wrestling exhibitions
	: Kiyoshi Shibata - Practiced under Kimura during traveling matches
	: Osamu Matsunami - Practiced with Kimura during his post-competitive judo years.
	: Mas Oyama - Though primarily a karateka, Oyama trained judo under Kimura and reached 4th dan. 
	: Masaki Nishimura - Olympic bronze medalist in judo, also trained under Kimura at Takushoku University 
	: Kaneo Iwatsuri - All-Japan Champion, another student from Kimura’s tenure at Takushoku University
	Trained by Kimura: Doug Rogers - While most of his training was done at the Kodokan, under instructors like Yoshimi Osawa and Masahiko Tomita, he also seni-privately trained under Masahiko Kimura. Rogers also trained under Hiroshi Nakamura and encouraged him to move to Canada.
	: Gozo Shioda - Founder of Yoshinkan Aikido, attended university with Kimura and was part of his broader martial arts circle
	: Carl Gotch (possible) - Shared techniques with Kimura in Japan
	: Takushoku University Judo Club - Kimura served as a judo instructor at his alma mater from 1960 until his death in 1993, formally teaching and mentoring many judoka during this period
	: Brazilian BJJ figures - Indirect technical inheritance after 1951 Gracie match
