	timeline
	title Timeline of Kenwa Mabuni
	Section Okinawa
	November 14 1889: Born on  Shuri, Okinawa (17th generation descendant of the famous warrior Uni Ufugusuku Kenyu)
	1902: At the age of 13, he began training in Shuri-te under Ankō Itosu (Known for his emphasis on kata and the development of modern karate training methods including hard linear movements.)
	: Learned the Pinan series developed by Itosu
	1908: Introduced to Naha-te by Chōjun Miyagi and began training under Kanryō Higaonna (Focused on breathing techniques and powerful close-range combat with soft circular techniques).
	: Learned Sanchin
	March 11 1915: Ankō Itosu dies at 84 years old
	October 1915: Kanryō Higaonna dies at 62 years old
	1915 - 1924: Trained under Seisho Aragaki (Introduces katas like Unshu and Sochin)
	: Trained under Wu Xianhui (Go Kenki)  (A Chinese White Crane master who influences Mabuni’s understanding of circular techniques and hand movements)
	1918: Started his career as a police officer, which allowed him to travel and train with various martial arts masters.
	Early 1920s : Likely met Kori Hisataka when Kaiso began training with Ankō Asato, leading to a strong and lasting friendship. Both Ankō Asato and Ankō Itosu were students of Sōkon Matsumura.
	1921: Demonstrated Karate to members of Japan’s Imperial Family during their visit to Okinawa
	Section Mainland Japan
	1924: He began teaching karate in Okinawa and mainland Japan.
	: Appointed as a Karate instructor at the Prefectural School of Fisheries and a police training school.
 	: Demonstrated Karate again for the Imperial Family during another visit
	1928: Moved to Osaka, Japan, to teach karate full-time in universities and police academies.
	1929: Established a dojo in Osaka and started teaching his unique style. Initially named it "Hanko-Ryu" (Half-Hard Style)
	1931: Founded the Shitō-ryū (糸東流) style of karate (combining the kanji characters of his two main teachers - 糸 (Shi) from Itosu and 東 (To) from Higaonna)
	1934: Establishes first Shitō-ryū Karate dojo in Osaka, called "Yoshukan" (Cultivation of Superior Skill and Spirit). 
	March 1939: Officially registered Shitō-ryū with the Dai Nippon Butokukai (national martial arts association). He was awarded the rank of "Grandmaster of Karate" (Karate Jutsu Renshi)
	1940s: Continued to teach and promote karate throughout Japan, influencing many students and other karate styles.
	: Shitō-ryū Karate becomes known for its vast kata repertoire (50+ katas including Aoyagi/Seiryuu and Meijou/Myoujo that were created by him for women's self-defense), adaptability, and blend of hard & soft techniques.
	May 23 1952: Passed away in Tokyo, Japan at the age of 62
