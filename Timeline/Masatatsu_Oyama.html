	timeline
	title Timeline of Masatatsu Oyama
	Section Early life
	July 27, 1923: Born Choi Yeong-eui in Gimje, Jeolla Province, Korea (under Japanese occupation)
	1931: Sent to live on his sister’s farm in Manchuria (China). Met and studied Southern Chinese Kempo under Lee Sang-chi (“Mr. Yi”)
	Section Martial Arts Training
	1937-39: Returns to Busan, South Korea; reportedly meets karate master Sou Ningzhu.
	April 1938: Following his brother, moved to Japan to train as an aviator in the Japanese army. Changed his name to Masutatsu Oyama (a Japanese reading of his Korean name).
	1940-41: Trained in Shotokan Karate under Gichin Funakoshi and his son Yoshitaka Funakoshi at Takushoku University. Reached Yondan
	: (around this time) Studied Judo, reached Yondan in 4 years
	1943: Later studied Goju-Ryu Karate under So Nei Chu, a Korean (or Chinese) master living in Japan.
	1945: Faced identity crises as a Korean expatriate in postwar Japan, So Nei Chu advised him to isolate himself in nature for ascetic training, to “find his true self.”
	April 1946: Enrolls in Physical Education at Waseda University
	October 1946-1947: He retreated to Mount Minobu (Yamanashi Prefecture) to do spiritual and physical training for 14 months
	Sept. 1947: Wins the first postwar All Japan Karate Championship in Kyoto.
	April 1948-50: Again returned to isolation on Mount Kiyosumi (Chiba Prefecture) for more intense solitary training (12+ hours/day) of Reading Musashi’s Book of Five Rings, Zen meditation, kata, and breaking rocks and trees.
	Section Creating his own path
	1950-51: Emerged from the mountains and started performing national demonstrations of breaking stones and ice blocks, punching through thick boards, fighting multiple opponents in succession, and the infamous bullfights (apparently killing 52 bulls), where he broke their horns barehanded. This gained him the nickname “The Godhand” (ゴッドハンド).
	: Began teaching in Tokyo
	1952-53: Conducted a U.S. tour, performing on television and challenging boxers and wrestlers. Gained global recognition as one of the strongest karate fighters alive.
	1953: Opened his first dojo in Mejiro, Tokyo, under the name “Oyama Dojo.”. The training emphasized bare-knuckle fighting, body conditioning, and spirit over comfort.
	1960: Officially established Kyokushin (極真空手) Karate — meaning “Ultimate Truth Karate.”. Introduced the 100-man kumite (百人組手) 
	1964: Founded the International Karate Organization (IKO)
	: Opened the Kyokushin Hombu Dojo (Headquarters).
	: Codified Kyokushin kata, kihon, and kumite standards.
	: Published his first major book, What is Karate?, which became an international bestseller.
	1965-69: Authored "This is Karate", "Advanced Karate", and "The Kyokushin Way".
	: Began touring worldwide — especially to Europe, the Americas, and the Middle East.
	: Kyokushin spread rapidly through Japan, South Africa, the Netherlands, the U.K., and Brazil.
	: Introduced knockdown tournaments — bare-knuckle, no-protective-gear combat judged on realism.
	1970: Hosted the 1st All Japan Open Karate Tournament in Tokyo.
	1972: Kyokushin’s reach expanded to over 120 countries
	1973: Appears in the film Bodyguard Kiba (Original title: Bodigâdo Kiba)
	: Appears in the film Bodyguard Kiba2 (Original title: Bodigâdo Kiba: Hissatsu sankaku tobi)
	1975: Organized the 1st World Open Karate Tournament in Tokyo — the first international full-contact karate championship
	: Film "The Karate Bullfighter" was released. Mas Oyama is portrayed by one of his students (Japanese actor Sonny Chiba)
	1976: Film "The Strongest Karate" was released
	1978: Appears in the film "Karate Daisenso".
	Section Later years
	1980-89: Continued to write and lecture on Bushido and the Way of Karate.
	: Kyokushin became the largest karate organization in the world, boasting over 12 million members.
	1990–1993: Despite declining health, Oyama continued to oversee tournaments and teaching at Hombu.
	: Designated Shokei Matsui (then 8th dan, one of his most loyal students) as his successor to lead the IKO.
	April 26, 1994: Died of lung cancer in Tokyo, Japan, Buried at Gokokuji Temple in Tokyo.
	: Posthumously honored as Sosai (総裁) — “Supreme Director”
	: His passing led to the fragmentation of the IKO into multiple branches (IKO1, IKO2, IKO3, Kyokushin-kan, So-Kyokushin, etc.), each claiming lineage from the founder.
	2004: Was portrayed by actor Dong-kun Yang in the South Korean biographical film "Fighter in the Wind".
