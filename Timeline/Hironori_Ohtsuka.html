	timeline
	title Timeline of Hironori Ohtsuka

	Section Early Life & Jūjutsu Training
	June 1, 1892: Born in Shimodate City (now Chikusei), Ibaraki Prefecture, Japan.
	1897: Begins martial arts training in Shinmei-ryu swordsmanship under his uncle.
	1905: Begins formal training in Shindō Yōshin-ryū jūjutsu under Tatsusaburō Nakayama, the 3rd-generation headmaster.
	1911: Enters Waseda University to study business administration, but continues his studies of Shindo Yoshin Ryu Jujutsu.
	1917: He later leaves the university due to his father's death to work at the Kawasaki Bank and practice medical techniques like bone setting
	: He is believed to have met Morihei Ueshiba Sensei
	June 1, 1920: Earns full Menkyo Kaiden (master license) in Shindō Yōshin-ryū. He succeeded his master's position as fourth Grand Master.

	Section Shotokan years
	July 1922: Met Gichin Funakoshi at his first public demonstration of Okinawan karate in Tokyo
	1922-1927: Became one of his earliest and most dedicated students(learned all fifteen kata). Accompanied Funakoshi in demonstrations and classes at universities.
	1925: His mother died, and he was left in a period of indecision about his career.
	1927: Leaves bank job in Shimodate, opens a medical practice to treat martial arts injuries, devotes more time to karate and jujutsu.
	1929: Begins experimenting with blending jūjutsu body movement (tai sabaki) with karate striking.

	Section Wadō-ryū years
	1931: Establishes his own dojo in Tokyo.
	: Begins formally teaching a hybrid system known as Wadō-ryū Jūjutsu-Kenpō.
	April 1, 1934: Officially separated from Funakoshi’s Shotokan organization due to differences in teaching methods and philosophy.
	1938: Blending his jūjutsu with Funakoshi’s Shotokan he creates the core syllabus of Wadō-ryū (和道流) “The Way of Harmony” Karate-dō.
	: He was awarded the rank of Renshi-go by the Japan Martial Arts Federation
	1940: Wadō-ryū was officially recognized by the Dai Nippon Butokukai
	1942: Receives the title Kyoshi from Dai Nihon Butokukai.

	Section Growth and Formalization
	1944-1945: (WWII) Continued limited teaching; dojos are affected by wartime conditions.
	1945-1950: Postwar reconstruction period—reopens dojos and rebuilds the organization.
	1952: Begins teaching at universities and police academies.
	1954-1956: Wadō-ryū spreads across Japan and begins early international outreach.
	1960: Wadō-ryū was introduced in Europe (especially the UK) by British servicemen and Japanese instructors.
	1964: Establishes the Japan Karatedo Federation (JKF) with other style heads.
	1966: Awarded the Kun Goto Suoku medal(Order of the Rising Sun, Fifth Class) from the Emperor of Japan for his contributions to martial arts
	1967: Publishes "Karate-Do, Volume 1" (focused on kata).
	1970: Publishes "Karate-Do, Volume 2" (focused on kumite).
	March 22, 1970: Presented with the key to the city of Niagara Falls, NY.
	1970-1979: Travels to Europe and North America to oversee Wadō-ryū development.
	1972: Designated Meijin (10th Dan) — the highest possible rank — by the Japan Karate Federation.

	Section Later years
	December 20, 1981: Steps down, nominates his son Jiro Ohtsuka (later known as Hironori Ōtsuka II) as successor of the Wado-Ryu organization.
	January 29, 1982: Passes away peacefully at age 89 in Japan.
