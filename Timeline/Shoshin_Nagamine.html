	timeline
	title Timeline of Shōshin Nagamine
	Section Early years
	July 15, 1907: Born in Tomari village, Naha, Okinawa, to Shoho and Gozei Nagamine.
	Section Karate training
	1910-1920: Studied under several prominent Tomari-te masters, gaining exposure to multiple lines of Okinawan karate.
	March 1923: Completed primary school; entered the 5-year Naha City Commercial School.
	1926: Developed severe gastroenteritis during the second year of high school.
	: Began self-imposed diet and karate training under neighbour Chojin Kuba.
	: Recovery led to improved health, joining and later leading his school’s karate club. Nicknamed “Chippaii Matsu” (“tenacious pine tree”).
	1928-1931: Graduated from High school
	: Relocated to Shuri to pursue karate full-time under Taro Shimabuku, Ankichi Arakaki and Zenryo Shimabukuro.
	: Conscripted into the Japanese army in the 47th Infantry Regiment
	: Fought in China for the Jinan incident
	: Honourable discharge in 1931.
	1930: Began organizing and systematizing what he had learned.
	: Presenting karate as budō, not merely physical techniques.
	1931-1933: Joined the Okinawan Police Department, first stationed at Kadena Police Station (Dec 1931–Aug 1936).
	: Mostly trained with Chōtoku Kyan
	1936: Sent to Tokyo Metropolitan Police Academy; trained under Master Motobu Chōki—developed Yakusoku Kumite based on Motobu's teachings
	: Also influenced by the teachings of Sōkon Matsumura and Kōsaku Matsumora.
	1940: Achieved the title of Renshi (certified instructor) and earned 3rd Dan in Kendo, awarded in Kyoto.
	Section Forming his own path
	1945: Lived through the devastation of Okinawa during World War II. Reinforced his resolve to preserve traditional karate.
	1947: Founded Matsubayashi-ryū (“Matsu” (松) – Matsumura Sōkon, “Bai” (柏) – Matsumora Kōsaku) Karatedō
	1940-1950: Opened his dōjō in Okinawa and codified the Matsubayashi-ryū kata curriculum.
	Section Sharing his art
	1951: Promoted to Police Superintendent in Motobu; actively trained officers in karate.
	1952: Retired from the police force.
	1953: Returned to Naha; established Matsubayashi-Ryu Kodokan Karate and Ancient Martial Arts Studies dojo.
	: Served as Naha City assemblyman from 1953 to 1957.
	1960-1970: Matsubayashi-ryū spread internationally, especially to North America and Europe.
	1976: English edition of his book "The Essence of Okinawan Karate‑Do" published.
	1980-1990: Continued teaching and advising senior instructors on pre-war Okinawan karate culture. Maintained opposition to excessive sportification of karate.
	November 2, 1997: Passed away in Naha, Okinawa
	Late 1997: Leadership of Matsubayashi-ryū passed to his son Takayoshi Nagamine, who returned from the U.S.; served as sōke until his death in 2012.
	2000: "Tales of Okinawa's Great Masters", written by him and translated by Patrick McCarthy was published in English
